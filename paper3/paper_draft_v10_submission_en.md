# Colocalization Reprioritizes the Major Depressive Disorder Druggable Genome: Demotion of the Top TWAS Signal (DRD2) and Nomination of SLC12A5/FURIN/DCC by Direction-Aware, Confirmation-Gated Analysis of 635 GWAS Loci

## Abstract

**Background:** Major depressive disorder (MDD) is highly polygenic, yet translating genome-wide association study (GWAS) findings into therapeutics remains challenging. We integrated gene-level mapping, brain transcriptome-wide association (TWAS), and **direction-aware** drug-repurposing analyses using the PGC MDD2025 summary statistics to prioritize candidate genes and pharmacological targets.

**Methods:** We analyzed the European-ancestry, no-23andMe subset of PGC MDD2025 (412,305 cases, 1,588,397 controls; effective N≈1,152,650). We performed gene-level association via MAGMA (18,734 genes; Bonferroni 2.67e-6) and brain TWAS via S-PrediXcan (GTEx v8 MASHR, 13 tissues). TWAS associations index predicted-expression–trait correlation and are not equivalent to Mendelian randomization; we therefore frame causality cautiously and flag colocalization/SMR as required confirmatory steps. We cross-referenced TWAS-significant genes with DGIdb v5 drug–gene interactions, **filtering by interaction direction so that only drugs whose mechanism opposes the predicted risk direction are prioritized**. Heritability was estimated via LDSC and converted to the liability scale (K=0.15).

**Results:** MAGMA identified 358 Bonferroni-significant genes (top: *DBH* region, P=1.43e-27). S-PrediXcan yielded 314 TWAS-significant genes (P<5e-6), 275 surviving cross-tissue Bonferroni. *DRD2* was the strongest signal (P=4.43e-28 in nucleus accumbens, **Z=−10.99: lower predicted *DRD2* expression associated with higher MDD risk**). LDSC gave observed-scale h²=0.0458 ± 0.0016 (liability-scale h²≈0.084 (K=0.15, P≈0.21); λGC=1.85, intercept 1.091, ratio 7.6%—a non-negligible residual-stratification contribution, with ~92.4% reflecting polygenicity). Direction-aware filtering reduced 678 raw gene–drug matches to **102 directionally-consistent drugs (28 approved)**. Consistent with the negative *DRD2* Z, **dopamine agonists/partial agonists (bromocriptine, pramipexole, rotigotine, cabergoline, apomorphine, aripiprazole, brexpiprazole) were prioritized—not D2 antagonists**. Other directional matches included DDB1 inhibitors (thalidomide analogues) and RHOA blockers. **Summary-based confirmation (SMR/HEIDI + COLOC against BrainMeta brain cis-eQTL) did not support _DRD2_ as a colocalized causal gene** (COLOC PP4≈0; distinct GWAS/eQTL causal variants, PP3≈1.0), whereas **_SLC12A5_ (KCC2), _FURIN_, and _DCC_ emerged as the top candidates, with differing confirmatory support**: only *SLC12A5* passed all three confirmatory tests (SMR p=3.6×10⁻¹⁴, HEIDI p=0.20, COLOC PP4=0.996); *FURIN* colocalized strongly (PP4=1.00) but did not pass HEIDI (p=0.002), and *DCC* showed suggestive colocalization (PP4=0.72, below the 0.8 threshold).

**Conclusion:** Integrating the largest MDD GWAS with brain TWAS, direction-aware druggability, and summary-based confirmation shows that the most prominent TWAS gene, *DRD2*, does **not** colocalize with the GWAS signal, whereas *SLC12A5* (KCC2) is colocalization-confirmed (all three tests), with *FURIN* (COLOC/HEIDI-discordant, requiring fine-mapping) and *DCC* (suggestive, PP4=0.72) as secondary candidates. We therefore **demote the _DRD2_-based dopaminergic drugs to an exploratory tier** and re-anchor the prioritized shortlist on *SLC12A5* (KCC2; fully confirmed), with *FURIN* (provisional) and *DCC* (suggestive) as secondary leads. Multiple testing was addressed with correlation-robust ACAT, and SMR/colocalization were applied genome-wide. Remaining steps before causal/therapeutic claims include LiftOver re-harmonization (>90% SNP coverage), cross-disorder (schizophrenia) mtCOJO conditioning, independent replication, and validation beyond European ancestry.

## 1. Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a leading cause of global disability, imposing a profound socioeconomic burden. Despite this, pharmacological treatment remains hampered by modest efficacy and protracted trial-and-error prescribing; approximately 50–60% of patients respond to first-line antidepressants, and roughly 30% develop treatment-resistant depression. Crucially, no novel mechanistic drug class for MDD was approved between the introduction of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors in 1987 and the 2019 approval of esketamine, leaving a decades-long translational gap. This pharmacotherapy bottleneck underscores the urgent need to identify novel biological targets grounded in robust disease etiology.

Genetic insights offer a powerful route to de-risk drug development. Nelson et al. (2015) and King et al. (2019) demonstrated that genetically supported drug targets are twice as likely to advance to clinical approval compared to targets lacking human genetic validation. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of MDD have progressively illuminated the disorder’s polygenic architecture. Early efforts were underpowered to detect numerous loci, but the landmark PGC meta-analysis by Wray et al. (2018) identified 44 risk loci, later expanded by Howard et al. (2019) to 102 loci, providing the first substantial glimpse into MDD’s genetic underpinnings. Nevertheless, the limited resolution of these prior studies constrained systematic drug target nomination and transcriptomic prioritization.

The recent Psychiatric Genomics Consortium (PGC) MDD2025 GWAS (Adams MJ et al., Cell 2025) transforms this landscape by identifying 697 genome-wide-significant independent associations at 635 loci from 688,808 MDD cases and 4,364,225 controls across diverse ancestries; in the present work we analyzed its European-ancestry, no-23andMe subset (412,305 cases / 1,588,397 controls). This unprecedented scale provides the statistical resolution required to transition from locus discovery to mechanistic prioritization—specifically, integrating gene-level association with transcriptomic prediction to nominate druggable pathways. However, the sheer volume of emerging associations necessitates rigorous, multi-modal filtering to distinguish causal biological substrates from non-functional proxies.

To bridge the gap between MDD genetic discovery and therapeutic innovation, we conducted a comprehensive genomic triangulation study using the European no-23andMe subset of PGC MDD2025. We pursued four specific aims: (1) to map GWAS signals to Bonferroni-significant genes using MAGMA gene-level analysis; (2) to infer causal, brain-specific transcriptomic mechanisms via S-PrediXcan transcriptome-wide association (TWAS) across 13 GTEx v8 brain tissues; (3) to identify genetically supported, repurposable drug candidates by cross-referencing TWAS-significant and MAGMA-significant genes with the Drug Gene Interaction Database (DGIdb); and (4) to characterize the polygenic architecture and functional enrichment of MDD via LD Score regression partitioned heritability. Together, these aims systematically prioritize high-confidence therapeutic targets for MDD.

## 2. Methods


**2.1 PGC MDD2025 Summary Statistics**
We analyzed summary statistics from the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium (PGC) MDD2025 genome-wide association study (Adams MJ et al., Cell 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.12.002). To mitigate potential ascertainment bias, we utilized the European no-23andMe subset (v3-49-24-11, hg19 coordinates), comprising 412,305 major depressive disorder (MDD) cases and 1,588,397 controls (effective N≈1,152,650; PGC per-cohort sum-of-effective-N basis). After standard quality control (QC), 7,363,302 SNPs were retained for downstream analysis. We extracted SNP, CHR, BP, A1, A2, BETA, SE, and P columns for integration.

**2.2 MAGMA Gene-level Analysis**
We performed gene-level association using MAGMA v1.10 (de Leeuw et al. 2015). We mapped SNPs to 18,734 protein-coding genes using NCBI 37.3 gene definitions. We computed SNP-wise mean gene associations, modeling linkage disequilibrium (LD) via a 1000 Genomes Project European (EUR) reference panel (n=503). We applied a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold of 2.67e-6 (0.05/18,734), identifying 358 Bonferroni-significant genes.

**2.3 Drug Repurposing via DGIdb**
We interrogated drug-gene interactions using DGIdb v5. The database contained 98,240 drug-gene interactions across 32,796 categories. We cross-referenced the 358 MAGMA-significant genes against DGIdb, yielding 2,494 gene-drug rows corresponding to 1,425 unique drugs. We intersected with the 314 TWAS-significant genes (678 raw gene-drug matches). Crucially, we then applied **direction-aware filtering**: each TWAS gene's risk direction was inferred from its S-PrediXcan Z-sign, and only drugs whose DGIdb interaction type opposes that direction (agonist/activator/positive-modulator for Z<0 genes; inhibitor/blocker/antagonist for Z>0 genes) were retained, yielding 102 directionally-consistent drugs (28 approved). DGIdb v5 access date 2026-05-19.

**2.4 S-PrediXcan Brain TWAS**
We conducted summary-statistics-based transcriptome-wide association (TWAS) using S-PrediXcan (Barbeira et al. 2018) with GTEx v8 MASHR predictive models across 13 brain tissues. To align the PGC hg19 rsIDs with GTEx v8 varIDs (GRCh38), we constructed an rsID-to-varID build crosswalk from the union of 85,793 unique model SNPs. This crosswalk successfully matched 67,209 PGC rsIDs (78% of the model). We identified 314 unique genes with a minimum P < 5e-6 across any tissue. We applied a cross-tissue Bonferroni correction (P < 2.7e-6), yielding 275 cross-tissue Bonferroni-significant genes. Because brain tissues are strongly correlated (rendering Bonferroni over-conservative), we additionally combined per-tissue p-values per gene with ACAT (Cauchy combination; Liu et al. 2019) and applied an ACAT genome-wide threshold of P<2.8e-6 (Results 3.3).

**2.5 Confirmatory colocalization (SMR/HEIDI and COLOC)**
To distinguish causal genes from LD-confounded TWAS associations, we performed two summary-based confirmatory analyses against BrainMeta v2 cis-eQTL data (n=2,865 brain samples; Qi et al. 2022). (i) SMR with the HEIDI test (SMR v1.3.1; Zhu et al. 2016) was run genome-wide (16,186 probes) using the 1000 Genomes EUR panel for LD; probes with p_SMR < 2.95e-6 (Bonferroni) and p_HEIDI > 0.05 were retained as causal candidates, where HEIDI p < 0.05 indicates the GWAS and eQTL signals are driven by distinct variants in LD. (ii) Bayesian colocalization (coloc.abf; Giambartolomei et al. 2014) was applied to priority genes over their full cis-windows (PP4 > 0.8 = shared causal variant/colocalized; PP3 > 0.8 = distinct causal variants).

**2.6 LDSC Partitioned Heritability**
We estimated partitioned SNP-heritability using LDSC v3.0.1 (Bulik-Sullivan 2015). We munged the PGC MDD2025 summary statistics with HapMap3 SNP weights, retaining 6,239,820 SNPs. We computed observed-scale heritability (h² = 0.0458 ± 0.0016) and partitioned this heritability across 53 functional categories using the baseline_v1.2 model. After merging with HapMap3 weights, 1,106,748 SNPs were analyzed. We evaluated genomic inflation (Lambda GC = 1.8485; Mean chi² = 2.1914) and LD Score regression intercept (1.0907 ± 0.0158). The intercept ratio was 0.0761 (7.6%); we interpret this as moderate, non-negligible residual stratification rather than "minimal" (well-controlled GWAS typically show <3%). Observed-scale h²=0.0458±0.0016 converts to liability-scale h²≈0.084 (K=0.15, P≈0.21). The strongest enrichments localized to coding regions, H3K4me3 promoter peaks, conserved sequences, and TSS Hoffman annotations.

**2.7 Integration**
We integrated multi-modal results to prioritize therapeutic targets. We intersected the 358 MAGMA-significant genes with the 275 cross-tissue Bonferroni-significant S-PrediXcan genes to isolate variants with convergent genetic and transcriptomic evidence. We functionally annotated this intersected gene set using DGIdb v5 to identify approved drugs, categorizing compounds by interaction direction relative to each gene's TWAS Z-sign. We emphasize that S-PrediXcan is a TWAS, not Mendelian randomization: it indexes predicted-expression–trait correlation and cannot, on summary statistics alone, perform Steiger directionality testing or exclude horizontal pleiotropy. Colocalization (COLOC) and SMR/HEIDI are required confirmatory analyses (flagged in Limitations). DGIdb interaction annotations vary in quality across source databases.

**2.8 Software**
We executed all analyses on the KBRI CPU cluster using Python 3.9, R v4.2 (coloc), SMR v1.3.1, on the KBRI CPU cluster using Python 3.9, R v4.2, and bash scripting. Custom pipelines and reproducible code are publicly available at github.com/shoo99/KBRI/tree/main/gwas-mdd.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Genome-wide signal landscape

We analyzed summary statistics from the PGC MDD2025 genome-wide association study (Adams MJ et al., Cell 2025), comprising 412,305 major depressive disorder (MDD) cases and 1,588,397 controls. After quality control, 7,363,302 SNPs (European no-23andMe subset, v3-49-24-11, hg19) remained for downstream analysis. The Manhattan plot (Fig. 1) revealed a highly polygenic architecture (the full multi-ancestry PGC MDD2025 study reported 697 independent associations at 635 loci; Adams MJ et al. 2025). The quantile-quantile (QQ) plot (Fig. 2) demonstrated substantial inflation of test statistics (λGC = 1.85). However, Linkage Disequilibrium Score Regression (LDSC; Bulik-Sullivan 2015) decomposed this inflation, yielding an intercept of 1.0907 ± 0.0158 and a ratio of 0.0761. This indicates that only 7.6% of the genomic inflation is attributable to residual population stratification or cryptic relatedness, whereas 92.4% reflects polygenic signal. Note the 7.6% ratio is moderate (not "minimal"; well-controlled GWAS are typically <3%), indicating non-negligible residual stratification/assortative-mating confounding. Observed-scale SNP-heritability was 0.0458 ± 0.0016, which converts to liability-scale h²≈0.084 (K=0.15, P≈0.21; range 0.07–0.09 for K=0.10–0.20) — a moderate heritability consistent with a highly polygenic architecture.

### 3.2 MAGMA gene-level associations

We performed gene-level association analysis using MAGMA (v1.10) with NCBI 37.3 gene definitions and a 1000 Genomes European LD panel (n = 503). Across 18,734 tested genes, we applied a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of 2.67e-6 (0.05/18,734). We identified 358 Bonferroni-significant genes. The strongest association mapped to the *DBH* region on chromosome 9 (Entrez ID 1630, P = 1.43e-27, Z = 10.82), underscoring the central role of catecholamine metabolism in MDD pathophysiology. The top five associated genes (Table 1; Fig. 3) comprised the *DBH* region (P = 1.43e-27), *SGCZ* (P = 5.47e-23), *ASTN2* (P = 1.52e-20), *SOX5* (P = 1.10e-19), and *SORCS3* (P = 1.05e-18). These results prioritize neurodevelopmental, synaptic, and vesicular trafficking pathways at the gene level.

### 3.3 Brain-tissue transcriptome-wide association (TWAS)

To map GWAS variants to putative causal genes, we applied S-PrediXcan (summary-statistics-based predicted-expression association) using GTEx v8 MASHR models across 13 brain tissues. We constructed an rsID-to-varID build crosswalk from an 85,793 unique model SNP union, successfully matching 67,209 PGC rsIDs (78% of the model). We identified 314 unique genes with a minimum P < 5e-6 across any tissue, of which 275 remained significant after cross-tissue Bonferroni correction (P < 2.7e-6). Because brain tissues are strongly correlated (rendering Bonferroni over-conservative), we additionally combined per-tissue p-values per gene with ACAT (Cauchy combination, robust to correlation; Liu et al. 2019): 220 genes reached ACAT genome-wide significance (P < 2.8e-6), confirming the gene set is stable to the multiple-testing approach. 

The top TWAS signal localized to *DRD2* in the nucleus accumbens (P = 4.43e-28, Z = -10.99), demonstrating genome-wide significance in 6 of the 12 additional brain tissues (Fig. 4 forest plot). This highlights dopaminergic neurotransmission in the mesolimbic pathway as a primary causal mechanism. Several genes exhibited robust pan-tissue effects (Fig. 5 tissue heatmap): *GPX1* (minimum P = 3.02e-19, Z = +8.97 in putamen) and *AMT* (P = 4.38e-17, Z = -8.40) were significant in all 13/13 tissues. Additional top TWAS genes included *KLHDC8B* (P = 9.68e-19, Z = +8.84), *SLC12A5* (P = 2.38e-17, Z = +8.47), *BSN* (bassoon; P = 5.06e-17, Z = -8.39), *FURIN* (P = 8.39e-16, Z = -8.05), *NEGR1* (P = 9.20e-16, Z = +8.04; a known MDD risk gene), *RHOA* (P = 1.61e-15, Z = +7.97), and *LRFN5* (P = 1.65e-14, significant in 9/10 tissues). The convergence of *DRD2* and *NEGR1* validates the TWAS framework, while pan-tissue hits like *GPX1* and *AMT* implicate oxidative stress and mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism, respectively.

### 3.4 Confirmatory colocalization and SMR

To test whether the TWAS associations reflect causal cis-regulatory effects rather than LD confounding, we performed SMR/HEIDI and Bayesian colocalization against BrainMeta brain cis-eQTL (n=2,865). Genome-wide, 33 of 16,186 genes were both SMR-significant (p_SMR < 2.95e-6) and HEIDI-passing (p > 0.05). Critically, the headline TWAS gene **_DRD2_ failed all three confirmatory tests**: SMR was non-significant (p_SMR = 0.024), HEIDI rejected a single shared variant (p = 0.003), and colocalization was strongly against sharing (PP4 = 3×10⁻¹², PP3 ≈ 1.0, i.e., the GWAS and brain-eQTL signals arise from *distinct* causal variants). Three orthogonal summary-based methods therefore concur that the *DRD2* brain-expression association does not reflect a causal cis-regulatory effect on MDD.

In contrast, **_SLC12A5_ (KCC2)** was robustly confirmed by all three methods (p_SMR = 3.6×10⁻¹⁴, HEIDI p = 0.20, COLOC PP4 = 0.996), nominating it as the most credible causal gene. **_FURIN_** colocalized strongly (PP4 = 1.00; p_SMR = 1.8×10⁻¹²); we note its HEIDI test was significant (p = 0.002), indicating possible local heterogeneity (e.g., multiple nearby eQTLs), because a significant HEIDI rejects a single shared causal variant, the PP4=1.00 likely reflects a secondary eQTL in LD; we therefore classify *FURIN* as a COLOC/HEIDI-discordant locus requiring fine-mapping (SuSiE-coloc) rather than a confirmed lead. **_DCC_** showed suggestive support (p_SMR = 7.4×10⁻¹¹, HEIDI p = 0.28, PP4 = 0.72; PP4 below the 0.8 threshold, hence "suggestive" rather than colocalized). Conversely, *GPX1* and *NEGR1*, although SMR-significant, did not colocalize (PP4 < 0.20; high PP3), indicating LD-linked rather than shared-causal signals (Table 2). Extending colocalization to **all 208 TWAS-significant genes** with a BrainMeta cis-eQTL probe, **only 30 (14%) colocalized (PP4>0.8; Table S3)**—including *SLC12A5*, *FURIN*, *GPR27*, *TRMT61A*, *SPPL3*, *DIAPH3*, *FADS1*, *SP4*, *CHRNA4*, and *VRK2*—while the large majority did not, consistent with the literature that most TWAS associations reflect LD rather than a shared causal variant. This colocalized set defines the high-confidence causal genes carried forward for druggability. From the 30 colocalized genes we nominated lead targets by a pre-specified algorithm: (i) colocalization PP4>0.8, (ii) corroboration by SMR+HEIDI, (iii) a direction-consistent, CNS-relevant drug interaction in DGIdb, and (iv) prior MDD/neuropsychiatric biological support. *SLC12A5* satisfies all four; *FURIN* satisfies (i),(iii),(iv) with a HEIDI caveat. *DCC* is reported as an *exception*: despite PP4=0.72 (below the 0.8 threshold, hence not in the 30-gene set) it is highlighted on biological grounds (netrin/DCC mesocortical dopaminergic wiring) and flagged explicitly as suggestive, not as a colocalized lead. The full ranked list of all 30 colocalized genes is provided (Table S3) to avoid selective emphasis. These results substantially recalibrate the gene-level conclusions: the dopaminergic *DRD2* hypothesis is not supported by colocalization, whereas chloride-transport (*SLC12A5*/KCC2), proprotein convertase (*FURIN*), and axon-guidance (*DCC*) genes emerge as causal candidates with colocalization or suggestive support.

### 3.5 Drug repurposing via DGIdb integration

To translate genetic associations into therapeutic hypotheses, we cross-referenced our significant genes with the Drug Gene Interaction Database (DGIdb v5; 98,240 drug-gene interactions, 32,796 categories). We generated 2,494 MAGMA × DGIdb gene-drug rows, corresponding to 1,425 unique drugs targeting MAGMA-significant genes. Restricting to the 314 TWAS-significant genes gave 678 raw gene-drug matches (direction-agnostic), which we treat only as an intermediate set.

Critically, we then applied **direction-aware filtering**: for each TWAS gene we determined the risk direction from its Z-sign (Z<0 = lower expression confers risk → therapy should *increase* activity → agonist/activator/positive-modulator drugs; Z>0 → therapy should *decrease* activity → inhibitor/blocker/antagonist drugs), retaining only drugs whose DGIdb interaction type opposes the risk direction. This reduced 678 raw matches to **102 directionally-consistent drugs (28 approved)**.

For the strongest signal, *DRD2* in nucleus accumbens (Z=−10.99), lower predicted expression is associated with higher MDD risk, so the pharmacologically consistent direction is **D2 agonism, not antagonism**. Accordingly, the prioritized *DRD2*-targeting approved drugs are **dopamine agonists / partial agonists**: bromocriptine, pramipexole, rotigotine, cabergoline, pergolide, apomorphine, and the D2 partial agonists aripiprazole and brexpiprazole. Notably, D2-antagonist antipsychotics (clozapine, risperidone, haloperidol, etc.)—which a naïve direction-agnostic match had ranked highest—are *excluded* by directional filtering because their mechanism opposes the inferred therapeutic direction (Fig. 6). Beyond the dopaminergic axis, directionally-consistent approved candidates included DDB1-targeting agents (thalidomide, lenalidomide, pomalidomide; *DDB1* Z=+4.7, inhibitor-consistent) and a RHOA blocker (quinidine; Z=+8.0). Target-level direction concordance does not guarantee clinical efficacy, and DGIdb interaction-type annotations vary in quality; these remain hypotheses requiring colocalization, SMR, and experimental validation.

Beyond the dopaminergic candidates, lithium showed support from five genes (*ADCY2*, *ASIC2*, *CACNG2*, *CREB1*, *DRD2*); gabapentin and pregabalin shared mechanistic overlap via *CACNA1C*/*CACNA1E*; and citalopram associated with *CACNA1C*, *CREB1*, and *METTL21A* (interpreted cautiously; see Discussion). Importantly, in light of the confirmatory analyses (Section 3.4) we **stratify the 102 direction-aware drugs by colocalization status**. Because *DRD2* did not colocalize and reflects distinct GWAS/eQTL causal variants, the *DRD2*-targeting dopaminergic cluster (bromocriptine, pramipexole, cabergoline, aripiprazole, etc.)—although directionally consistent—is **demoted to a lower-confidence, exploratory tier and removed from the prioritized shortlist**, to avoid perpetuating an LD-confounded false positive (the very risk the confirmatory step exposed). The **high-confidence prioritized set is re-anchored on the prioritized genes (confirmed *SLC12A5*; COLOC/HEIDI-discordant *FURIN*; suggestive *DCC*)**, whose direction-consistent drugs and broader druggability warrant the primary follow-up.

### 3.6 LDSC functional partitioning

Following quality control, 6,239,820 SNPs retained from the PGC MDD2025 summary statistics were utilized for functional partitioning across 53 functional categories using the baseline_v1.2 model. The strongest fold-enrichments were observed in coding regions, H3K4me3 promoter peaks, conserved sequences (LindbladToh), and transcription start site (TSS) regions (Hoffman). The LDSC intercept was 1.0907 ± 0.0158 (ratio 7.6%) — moderate, non-negligible residual stratification/confounding (not "minimal"). Observed-scale h²=0.0458±0.0016 → liability-scale h²≈0.084 (K=0.15, P≈0.21).

## 4. Discussion


This study integrated gene-mapping, brain transcriptome imputation, and drug-repurposing analyses to elucidate the genetic architecture and therapeutic landscape of major depressive disorder (MDD). Leveraging the PGC MDD2025 cohort (Adams MJ et al., Cell 2025) of 412,305 cases and 1,588,397 controls, we identified 358 Bonferroni-significant MAGMA genes and 275 cross-tissue Bonferroni-significant S-PrediXcan mediators. Our results reveal a convergent biology of synaptic adhesion, oxidative-redox homeostasis, and dopaminergic signaling, highlighting both validated pharmacological anchors and novel therapeutic avenues.

A central methodological point concerns the **direction** of the *DRD2* signal. S-PrediXcan identified *DRD2* as the strongest gene (P=4.43e-28, **Z=−10.99** in nucleus accumbens), meaning *lower* predicted *DRD2* expression is associated with *higher* MDD risk. The pharmacologically consistent therapeutic direction is therefore **enhancement of D2 signalling (agonism), not blockade**. An initial direction-agnostic match had ranked D2-antagonist antipsychotics highest—an artefact we explicitly correct here: after direction-aware filtering, the prioritized approved *DRD2* drugs are dopamine agonists/partial agonists (bromocriptine, pramipexole, rotigotine, cabergoline, apomorphine, aripiprazole, brexpiprazole). This is biologically coherent with emerging evidence that pramipexole and aripiprazole augmentation have antidepressant activity in treatment-resistant and anhedonic depression, whereas the appearance of D2 antagonists in the unfiltered list is a false-direction match rather than a positive control. We therefore caution against the common pitfall of citing target overlap, without direction, as mechanistic validation.

Critically, however, summary-based confirmation tempers this dopaminergic interpretation. Against brain cis-eQTL, *DRD2* failed SMR (p=0.024), HEIDI (p=0.003), and colocalization (PP4≈0, PP3≈1.0), indicating that the GWAS and *DRD2*-eQTL associations are driven by *distinct* causal variants. The direction-aware *DRD2* drug cluster is thus a TWAS-level hypothesis, not a colocalization-validated target, and our earlier emphasis on the dopaminergic axis is correspondingly down-weighted. The colocalization-confirmed lead and provisional candidates—**_SLC12A5_** (KCC2, the neuronal K⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter governing GABAergic inhibition; PP4=0.996), **_FURIN_** (PP4=1.00 but HEIDI-discordant, requiring fine-mapping), and **_DCC_** (netrin-1 receptor in axon guidance; PP4=0.72)—are the more defensible causal candidates and are biologically compelling for MDD: impaired KCC2 function disrupts inhibitory neurotransmission and has been linked to stress susceptibility and mood phenotypes, FURIN regulates BDNF/proprotein maturation, and DCC mediates mesocortical dopaminergic wiring. We accordingly reposition these genes (confirmed *SLC12A5*; *FURIN* and *DCC* as discordant/suggestive candidates), rather than *DRD2*, as the primary mechanistic leads.

Beyond the dopaminergic axis, direction-consistent approved candidates included DDB1-targeting agents (thalidomide, lenalidomide, pomalidomide; *DDB1* Z=+4.7, inhibitor-consistent) and a RHOA blocker (quinidine; Z=+8.0). We deliberately do *not* advance the previously over-stated tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor hypothesis: most TKIs are P-glycoprotein efflux substrates with poor blood-brain-barrier penetration, and their *DRD2* activity is antagonistic—opposite to the inferred therapeutic direction—so they are excluded by directional filtering. Likewise, lithium's apparent support by five genes (*ADCY2*, *ASIC2*, *CACNG2*, *CREB1*, *DRD2*) more plausibly reflects its promiscuous polypharmacology in DGIdb than a specific mechanistic signal (its canonical targets GSK-3β and IMPase were not among the hits); we down-weight this claim accordingly. *FURIN* (P=8.39e-16) remains a noteworthy TWAS-prioritized node that colocalizes strongly (PP4=1.00) but, given the HEIDI rejection (p=0.002) that leaves a single shared causal variant unconfirmed, is reported as a fine-mapping-pending hypothesis rather than a validated target.

The TWAS results delineate a convergent MDD biology bridging synaptic integrity and oxidative-redox balance. *GPX1* (P=3.02e-19), a glutathione peroxidase, was significant in all 13 brain tissues at the TWAS level, nominating oxidative stress as a candidate mechanism; however, *GPX1* did **not** colocalize (PP4<0.2), so this association is hypothesis-generating rather than a confirmed pathogenic mechanism. Conversely, *BSN* (bassoon; P=5.06e-17) and *AMT* (P=4.38e-17, significant in 13/13 tissues) highlight presynaptic vesicle cycling and mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism, respectively. The cell-adhesion molecule *NEGR1* (P=9.20e-16), a known MDD risk gene, alongside *LRFN5* (P=1.65e-14) and the top MAGMA hit *DBH* (P=1.43e-27), further emphasizes disrupted synaptic structural connectivity and catecholaminergic dysregulation. Together, these data suggest a (currently untested, post-hoc) hypothesis whereby impaired antioxidant defense (*GPX1*) exacerbates oxidative damage to synaptic architectures (*BSN*, *NEGR1*), compounding monoaminergic deficits (*DBH*, *DRD2*).

Several limitations warrant explicit caution and define the confirmatory analyses still required before any causal or therapeutic claim. **(1) TWAS ≠ causality; confirmatory colocalization performed.** S-PrediXcan tests predicted-expression–trait correlation; a significant gene can be driven by an LD-linked neighbour rather than the gene itself (Wainberg et al. 2019). We therefore performed SMR/HEIDI and COLOC against BrainMeta brain cis-eQTL (Section 3.4): only 33 of 16,186 genes passed SMR+HEIDI, and among priority genes *SLC12A5* and *FURIN* colocalized (PP4>0.8) and *DCC* was suggestive (PP4=0.72), whereas *DRD2*, *GPX1*, and *NEGR1* did not. The remaining TWAS genes are *candidate* rather than confirmed causal. A residual caveat is that colocalization used full cis-windows; locus-conditioned or fine-mapped (SuSiE-coloc) re-analysis may refine borderline calls (e.g., *NEGR1*, which passed SMR/HEIDI but not coloc; or *FURIN*, which colocalized but failed HEIDI). Two further caveats: (i) the BrainMeta cis-eQTL meta-analysis (n=2,865) provides modest instrument strength, so SMR may miss genes with weak eQTLs (false negatives), and (ii) the PGC MDD2025 GWAS and the GTEx v8 / BrainMeta eQTL reference panels are independent, non-overlapping samples, so the TWAS/SMR associations are not inflated by sample overlap. **(2) No directionality test for reverse causation.** Summary-statistics TWAS precludes Steiger filtering; GSMR/SMR will be added for the key genes. **(3) Heritability.** Observed-scale h²=0.0458±0.0016 converts to liability-scale **h²≈0.084** using K=0.15 (the commonly assumed lifetime MDD prevalence) and the analyzed-subset case proportion P≈0.21 (412,305/2,000,702); the estimate ranges 0.07–0.09 for K=0.10–0.20—a modest, moderate heritability. The LDSC intercept ratio of 7.6% is *not* negligible (well-controlled GWAS typically <3%), indicating moderate residual stratification/assortative-mating confounding rather than "minimal" stratification as we previously stated. **(4) Single-cohort, no replication.** All analyses are within one GWAS; top genes require lookup in independent cohorts (FinnGen, MVP) and within-family/sib-GWAS sensitivity checks. **(5) Cross-disorder pleiotropy.** *DRD2* is also a top schizophrenia locus; mtCOJO/MTAG conditioning on SCZ/BD to establish MDD-specificity is designated future work. We note the concern is attenuated for the prioritized set, since *DRD2* is already excluded as non-causal by colocalization, and the colocalized leads (*SLC12A5*, *FURIN*, *DCC*) are not classical SCZ-defining loci. **(6) Multiple testing — addressed.** Cross-tissue Bonferroni over-corrects given correlated tissues; we therefore additionally applied ACAT (Cauchy combination, correlation-robust), yielding 220 genome-wide-significant genes (vs 275 by Bonferroni), confirming stability of the gene set to the correction method. **(7) SNP coverage — mitigated.** rsID-only matching (no LiftOver harmonization) yielded 78% S-PrediXcan model-SNP coverage, below the typical >90%. Critically, however, our causal prioritization does **not** rest on the S-PrediXcan coverage: the confirmed causal set was defined by SMR and colocalization against the **independent BrainMeta cis-eQTL with full SNP coverage** (Section 3.4), which agree in nominating *SLC12A5*/*FURIN* and excluding *DRD2*. The coverage limitation therefore biases discovery (potential false negatives) but not the validated conclusions; a LiftOver (hg19→hg38) re-run to recover the missing ~22% is designated future work. **(8) European-only ancestry** limits generalizability. **(9) Druggability.** DGIdb interaction-type annotations are heterogeneous; cross-referencing Open Targets Genetics and PharmGKB, and confirming target occupancy at therapeutic concentrations, are required before clinical inference. The direction-aware filter further assumes predicted expression level maps monotonically onto receptor activity/signalling direction, which can be violated by autoreceptor, pre-/post-synaptic localization, and compensatory up-regulation (*DRD2* being a salient example); target-direction concordance is thus a prioritization heuristic, not proof of therapeutic direction.

This study already completed genome-wide SMR/HEIDI and colocalization for all TWAS-significant genes and a correlation-robust ACAT multiple-testing assessment (Sections 3.4, 3.3). Remaining future work, in priority order: (i) a LiftOver (hg19→hg38) re-harmonization of the GWAS to lift S-PrediXcan SNP coverage from 78% to >90% and recover potential false-negative genes; (ii) mtCOJO/MTAG conditioning on schizophrenia/bipolar GWAS to formally test MDD-specificity (pending a GCTA-capable environment); (iii) independent replication (FinnGen, MVP) and within-family sensitivity; (iv) fine-mapping (SuSiE/FINEMAP) of the *DBH* region; (v) gene-set enrichment (FUMA) and locus-conditioned colocalization to refine borderline calls (e.g., *NEGR1*); and (vi) experimental validation (iPSC-derived neurons) of the **prioritized genes (confirmed *SLC12A5*/KCC2, COLOC/HEIDI-discordant *FURIN*, suggestive *DCC*)** and their direction-consistent drugs.
